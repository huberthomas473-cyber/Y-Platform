# Indigenous Land Recognition — verification log

**Ballot text:** "Should Indigenous peoples' ownership and stewardship of their traditional lands be legally recognized and enforceable?"
**Wording status:** passes rulebook §3.

## Claims shown in the app

| Claim | Source | Status | Checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| UNDRIP (2007) affirms Indigenous land rights with broad state endorsement | UN General Assembly A/RES/61/295 (13 Sep 2007), adopted 144–4; the four initial "no" states (AU, CA, NZ, US) later endorsed | ✅ Verified | 7 Aug 2026 |
| UNDRIP is non-binding | It is a UNGA declaration, not a treaty — un.org/development/desa/indigenouspeoples | ✅ Verified | 7 Aug 2026 |
| Indigenous peoples manage or hold tenure rights over more than a quarter of the world's land (~38M km², 87 countries) | Garnett et al. 2018, "A spatial overview of the global importance of Indigenous lands for conservation", *Nature Sustainability* 1:369–374 — nature.com/articles/s41893-018-0100-6 | ✅ Verified — exact figures | 7 Aug 2026 |
| That area intersects ~40% of terrestrial protected areas and ecologically intact landscapes | Same paper (abstract states ~40%) | ✅ Verified | 7 Aug 2026 |
| Enforceable legal title lags far behind | Supported by tenure literature (RRI tenure tracking); phrased qualitatively on purpose | ✅ Verified (framing) | 7 Aug 2026 |
| Stance: `partial` | Declaration without enforceability | ✅ Editorial judgment | 7 Aug 2026 |

**Caution for editors:** do NOT use the popular "Indigenous peoples protect 80% of the
world's biodiversity" figure — it has no traceable methodology and has been formally
debunked (see Fernández-Llamazares et al. 2021). Garnett 2018 is the defensible source.
